## Goal, topology and software boundary

One existing Zigbee network with >100 device records (~61 routers, ~44 end devices, one coordinator in archived Z2M database; record count does not prove simultaneous powered activity). Home Assistant OS on Raspberry Pi 5 with USB3 SSD, Zigbee2MQTT 2.14.0 / pinned herdsman 10.9.1 and SONOFF Dongle Max EFR32MG24 Ember/EZSP coordinator (historical captured EmberZNet 9.1.1/EZSP19, but a dated firmware observation is not an independently verified current binary). Zigbee radio channel 11; keep extPAN/PAN/coordinator IEEE/keys, pairing, production firmware, TX, channel and the *sole existing Zigbee2MQTT serial owner* unchanged during diagnostics. Original dongle position was ~10 cm from Pi/SSD; it was physically relocated, with no observed RF-counter improvement in the matched windows. The former SMLIGHT SLZB-06P7/TI Z-Stack reportedly handled the same network better, but different stack counters are not directly comparable; historical end-to-end evidence is incomplete. Workroom left/right dimmers, bedroom bulbs and living-room circle lights are sometimes deliberately unpowered: do not mistake their silence for RF failure. Avoid publishing private room-to-device maps or their IEEE/NWK IDs.

Practical fault reports include intermittent real ZCL set failures (especially historically problematic bulbs/dimmers), route-error bursts, router management-query/map failures and repeated neighbor changes. A 33-read canary on 11 selected powered router endpoints is *not* a representative test of 100+ devices, group commands, all hops, or long-tail command reliability.

## Reproducible captures, counter definitions and measured outcomes

The coordinator exposes `ezspNeighborCount` and `ezspGetNeighbor(index)` through the running Z2M owner, giving `shortId`, `longId` (private), `averageLqi`, `inCost`, `outCost` and `age`; its configured neighbor capacity is **26**, occupied **26/26** at all sampled endpoints. Non-clearing `ezspReadCounters` yields named counters including `MAC_TX_UNICAST_SUCCESS`, `MAC_TX_UNICAST_FAILED`, `MAC_TX_UNICAST_RETRY`, `PHY_CCA_FAIL_COUNT`, `NEIGHBOR_ADDED`, `NEIGHBOR_REMOVED`, `NEIGHBOR_STALE`, `APS_DATA_TX_UNICAST_SUCCESS/FAILED`, `ROUTE_DISCOVERY_INITIATED`, packet-buffer/ASH/queue errors. The stock herdsman watchdog clears counters about hourly: do **not** use raw totals across a clear, and a counter decrease after an intervening clear invalidates an ordinary delta. The original post-move attempt produced **33/33 genuine endpoint.read ZCL responses**, but ten counters simultaneously decreased at the scheduled-clear boundary; its RF window is intentionally **rejected** and retained. The valid A/B/B-repeat windows have unchanged Z2M owner epoch, no observed clear markers, comparable MAC traffic, a fixed 11-device eligible cohort and verified temporary extension cleanup.

No observed packet allocation, broadcast table, ASH/framing or queue-limit exhaustion during these windows. MAC failed **frames** are not an APS or household-command failure count; aggregate MAC retries, CCA failures and neighbor events are also different phenomena. Relocation did not demonstrate improvement, but because A/B occurred at different times and position affects antenna geometry as well as proximity to USB3, neither a detrimental relocation effect nor absence of USB3 contribution is proven. All three 11-router ZCL canaries succeeded despite substantial aggregate MAC errors and churn. Full placement report: [issue #27 after evidence](../evidence/issue-27/R60_PLACEMENT_AFTER_20260922.md); detailed neighbor analysis: [issue #28 findings](../evidence/issue-28/R60_NEIGHBOR_MECHANISMS_20260922.md).

## Neighbor and route-cache mechanism clues (NOT proven causes)

At the beginning of the three valid five-minute windows, neighbors with known outgoing cost versus `outCost=0` (unknown two-way cost) were **25/1, 22/4, 22/4**. Of neighbors subsequently absent from the respective endpoint snapshots, **1/5, 1/3, 3/5** had initially `outCost=0`; **none** was already `age>6` at the first endpoint. For the three starts combined, **5 of 9 initially unknown-outgoing-cost neighbor observations** disappeared by the endpoint, compared with **8 of 69 with known outgoing cost**. These are dependent *snapshot observations*, not 78 independent routers, an eviction-risk estimate, or proof the link changed before departure. Initial median incoming LQI of departing versus retained neighbors was **104 vs 120 (A), 91 vs 103 (B), 71 vs 103 (B repeat)**. Across the six valid endpoint snapshots, **39 distinct identities** were observed in a table that holds 26 at a time; six identities appeared in an in–out–in *sample pattern*, not necessarily six actual eviction events. Counter-level neighbor add/remove events exceeded distinct endpoint changes: intermediate transitions are unobserved.

Cached concentrator source routes pointed to a first hop *absent from the same endpoint neighbor table* for **11/81→9/81 (A), 4/82→3/83 (B), 8/83→5/83 (B repeat)**. At the starting snapshots, **9, 1, 5** source-route entries respectively used a cached first hop that subsequently disappeared from the endpoint neighbor table. A cached route table is not a timestamped air trace and missing neighbor membership alone is not proof of failure; check the meaning/direction of `closerIndex`, index sentinel `255`, route aging and the distinction between destination/first hop before proposing a fix. The *source-route capacity itself is not exhausted*. Raw IEEE, short-address mappings, friendly names and raw snapshots remain in private issue-specific evidence on the authorized machine. The public analyzer contains only aggregate results: [source](../deploy/r60_neighbor_mechanisms.py), [tests](../tests/test_r60_neighbor_mechanisms.py).

## Technical mechanisms worth checking independently

**1. Neighbor admission/eviction and bidirectional feedback.** Silicon Labs describes a ~16-second jittered, unacknowledged one-hop Link Status broadcast and `inCost`/`outCost`-dependent bidirectional recognition. `outCost=0` is *unknown*, not directly low RSSI. Dense network neighbor replacement may be normal; a route request through an unrecognized/one-way peer may be discarded. Could links be oscillating when the 26-entry table is saturated, or do marginal links and concurrent RF contention explain both? What exact EmberZNet 9.1.1 policy, timer and callback would expose a reason *without modifying NCP production firmware*? https://docs.silabs.com/zigbee/8.2.0/zigbee-fundamentals/04-zigbee-routing-concepts

**2. MAC ACK loss versus CCA/collision/interferer.** The MAC-fail counter cannot identify an individual next hop; APS message-sent callback reports logical destination and status, not necessarily the actual MAC next hop. Distinguish a busy medium, missed ACK due to near–far/asymmetric RF, coordinator antenna effects, Wi-Fi coexistence on Zigbee channel 11, and a specific poor router. A Wi-Fi SSID/channel survey is not a Zigbee spectrum analyzer; an independently clock-aligned receive-only 802.15.4 sniffer might help but a single vantage cannot establish a transmitter’s own CCA or all apartment RF conditions. Counter definitions: https://docs.silabs.com/zigbee/9.0.2/sisdk-ezsp-reference-guide/03-protocol-format

**3. Many-to-one / source-route asymmetry and route maintenance.** Differentiating concentrator→device cached source route from device→concentrator many-to-one return path is essential. Is a cached first-hop-not-neighbor entry normal for this Ember implementation? Is a historical route record being used after link changes? Are concentrator MTO requests actually propagated through all downstream routers? Can a stale IEEE↔NWK mapping or address-conflict burst independently explain a route error? API background: https://docs.silabs.com/zigbee/9.1.0/zigbee-stack-api/source-route

**4. On-MG24 capacity/stack implementation.** A 26-entry direct-neighbor table does not imply 60+ routers in the *whole network* require 60 direct slots. A historical P009/stock ELF audit reports 27 physical 18-byte neighbor slots (486 B), with 26 configured active entries; the *currently running firmware binary* is not established by that historical ELF. Changing a constant alone may leave index widths, link-status fragmentation/entry enumeration, Mgmt_Lqi pagination, neighbor aging/admission, security and routing-library assumptions broken. See [firmware feasibility #20](https://github.com/analienx/Sonoff-Dongle-Max/issues/20), [complete implementation #22](https://github.com/analienx/Sonoff-Dongle-Max/issues/22), and [draft PR #26](https://github.com/analienx/Sonoff-Dongle-Max/pull/26). Ask whether **26 direct neighbors with better policy/link quality** can serve 60+ mesh routers; demand SDK-generated linker/map and library/source ownership before 60-slot feasibility claims.

**5. Queue, transport and management requests.** The prior full map and some router Mgmt_Lqi/Mgmt_Rtg queries failed, but do not infer RF failure from a UI timeout without checking Z2M request lifecycle, single-flight queue, retry and cancellation. No packet/ASH resource failures in the short A/B samples does not exclude earlier burst pressure. Separate NCP `BUSY`, actual frame delivery, router non-response and host-side timeout.

**Algorithm inspiration, not a proposed drop-in fix:** De Couto et al., *A High-Throughput Path Metric for Multi-Hop Wireless Routing*, 2003, DOI 10.1145/938985.939000, emphasizes forward/reverse delivery and retransmission. This is 802.11 research; do not claim per-link Zigbee ETX from aggregate coordinator counters or move network routing to HA. For other papers, identify actual stack/version/network size, experimental topology, workload, link direction, uncertainty and transferable measurements.

## Questions to answer in your issue comment (specific and falsifiable)

1. What is the most informative **alternative root-cause mechanism** consistent with 26/26 occupancy, high add/remove counts, unknown outgoing cost among subsequently absent peers, non-exhausted source-route capacity and 33/33 canary successes? What observation would disprove it?
2. Does EmberZNet 9.1.1 specifically couple the neighbor table to **Link Status TX/RX, many-to-one route admission, incoming route request filtering, source-route first-hop validity or security**? Name the actual SDK source/symbol/version or clearly mark proprietary assumptions. Does a 26→60 table actually solve *total-mesh* reliability?
3. Are the reported `outCost=0`, `age`, `averageLqi`, `inCost` and `closerIndex` semantics interpreted correctly for Ember/EZSP19? Are there sentinel values, index ranges, wraparound, or stack-specific counter caveats that would invalidate the offline analyzer?
4. How can we **passively observe a true failing next hop** and distinguish PHY CCA, MAC ACK, NWK route failure and APS/ZCL timeout using the pinned herdsman/Ember owner or an independent receiver? Identify concrete callback hooks and whether they observe the physical next hop versus the APS destination.
5. What bounded **10-minute time-series sampling cadence** gives useful temporal resolution relative to the roughly 16-second Link Status period without saturating the serial request queue? Should a low-cost neighbor-count/table read be performed every 20–30 seconds with counter snapshots only at safe boundaries, or is a better passive event source available? State overhead, jitter, hourly-clear handling, owner epoch and cleanup requirements. **A requested ten-minute capture must not be reported as executed without actual logs.**
6. How would you design a natural-traffic failure correlation using existing HA/Z2M logs and only approved read-only probes, so a problem device cannot be misclassified because its power was intentionally off? Specify negative controls, timestamp alignment, address-change handling, sample size and what would change the conclusion.
7. Is there a practical, documented **non-firmware configuration or physical-network remedy** more causally justified than blind TX changes, channel switches, mass re-pairing or disabling routing on many sockets? Define safe reversible A/B and failure criteria.
8. Which open-source implementation or paper offers a reproducible **admission hysteresis, bidirectional-link reliability, route aging or congestion-discrimination algorithm**, and what would have to be proven before adapting it to the proprietary/partly precompiled on-MG24 stack?
