This was a **bounded, read-only application-command test**, not a production firmware candidate or a deliberate link-loss experiment. The one historical HA log archive remains unchanged; no second broad HA log-collection pass.

Executed `deploy/r60_live_coordinator_read.py` on authorized Zephyrus using its host-key-verified `ha` alias. A separate ephemeral MQTT *client* in the existing Z2M container published **one** standard `zigbee2mqtt/HallBulb1/get` request containing `{"brightness":""}`. MQTT credentials were read locally inside the container and never printed/passed as command arguments. The script limits requests to two, 28 seconds, no retained publish, and reports only sanitized status. No pairing, full map, power interruption, TX/channel/NVM, add-on restart, external-extension installation, firmware flash, or Zigbee2MQTT database/config mutation.

**Observed output:** `status=fresh_state_received`; `requests_sent=1`; `fresh_nonretained_brightness_response=true`; `first_response_ms=141`; `elapsed_ms=143`; `route_error_log_messages=0`; `target_error_log_messages=0`; `same_owner_epoch=true`; `one_owner_preflight=true`. MQTT publish success alone is not ZCL success.

**Conclusion:** live coordinator use and a successful application-level path were demonstrated. **Automatic recovery after a broken many-to-one route was NOT tested or proven**, because this one-shot non-disruptive request encountered no observed failure and no intermediate-link interruption was induced. The older 371 many-to-one error lines remain relevant; do not claim this result fixes their underlying cause or proves/disproves 26-neighbor saturation. No coordinator settings were changed. The next live recovery stimulus must be explicitly bounded and must preserve household availability; do not improvise with a router power-cut, network-wide map, repeat MTORR flood or UART takeover.
